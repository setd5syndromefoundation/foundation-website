SETD5 Syndrome Foundation

Bylaws revisions — summary of changes

Prepared for presentation at the April 29, 2026 inaugural board meeting

The following changes were made to the draft bylaws before presentation for adoption. All changes reflect standard governance practice for a founding nonprofit board. No substantive governance rights were altered.

1
Officer title: Chair updated to President/Board Chair
Article II §5 · Article III §§1, 2(a), 2(b), 2(e) · Article IV §2 · Article V §§1, 5 · Article VI §2 · Article VII §1 · COI Policy Article 3.3

The original draft referred to the role as "Chair" in some sections and "President" in others, creating inconsistency in what the formal title actually is. Combining them into President/Board Chair resolves that inconsistency and establishes a single clear title throughout. The bylaws already assigned the Chair full organizational leadership authority in the absence of a paid Executive Director, so President/Board Chair more accurately reflects what the role actually is rather than expanding its authority.

Before
"The officers of the Corporation shall be a Chair, a Secretary, and a Treasurer."
After
"The officers of the Corporation shall be a President/Board Chair, a Secretary, and a Treasurer."
2
Optional officer title: Vice Chair updated to Vice President/Vice Chair
Article III §§1, 2(b)

For consistency with the updated primary officer title, the optional Vice Chair position has been retitled Vice President/Vice Chair. The duties of the role are unchanged.

Before
"The Board may elect additional officers — including a Vice Chair, President, or Executive Director..."
After
"The Board may elect additional officers — including a Vice President/Vice Chair or Executive Director..."
3
Meeting frequency increased from two to four per year
Article II §7

The original draft set a minimum of two regular meetings per year, which is the legal floor under Virginia law. Four meetings per year reflects standard governance practice for an active founding board without paid staff, providing enough touchpoints for meaningful decision-making without overburdening volunteers. The bylaws have been updated accordingly.

Before
"The Board shall hold at least two (2) regular meetings per year..."
After
"The Board shall hold at least four (4) regular meetings per year..."
4
Executive Director transition: President title relinquished, Board Chair title retained
Article III §2(e)

The original language said that upon hiring an Executive Director, the Chair would "relinquish the President title." With the updated combined title of President/Board Chair, this provision needed clarification: upon hiring an ED, the President/Board Chair steps back from the President (organizational leadership) role but continues to serve as Board Chair (governance role). This reflects standard nonprofit practice where the Board Chair and the Executive Director serve distinct functions.

Before
"Upon hiring an Executive Director, the Chair shall relinquish the President title unless otherwise determined by the Board."
After
"Upon hiring an Executive Director, the President/Board Chair shall relinquish the President title and serve as Board Chair only, unless otherwise determined by the Board."
5
Budget operating responsibility assigned to Treasurer only
Article VII §1

The original draft assigned budget operating responsibility jointly to the President/Board Chair and the Treasurer. Assigning it to a single officer creates clearer accountability and is standard nonprofit practice. The Treasurer (or Executive Director if one is employed) now holds that responsibility. The President/Board Chair's financial role remains at the Board level: reviewing reports, facilitating discussion, and participating in budget approval votes.

Before
"The President/Board Chair, or Executive Director if one is employed, and the Treasurer shall operate within the approved budget..."
After
"The Treasurer, or Executive Director if one is employed, shall operate within the approved budget..."
6
Written consent threshold changed from unanimous to majority
Article II §10

The original draft required unanimous written consent for action without a meeting, which is Virginia's default rule. For a three-person founding board, unanimous consent means any single director can block action simply by not responding. Majority written consent is the more practical standard for a small board and is broadly used by nonprofits of this size. All directors are still required to receive notice of the proposed action and have the opportunity to respond before the action is taken — the change affects the threshold for approval, not the notice requirement. Each director's response is recorded and filed with the minutes.

Before
"...may be taken without a meeting if all directors consent to the action in writing..."
After
"...may be taken without a meeting if a majority of directors consent to the action in writing. All directors must receive notice of the proposed action and have the opportunity to respond..."
7
Standing program committees formally established; Community Committee added; working group structure added
Article V §2

The original draft listed a Research Committee as a recommended standing committee but did not formally establish it as a founding committee, and did not reference the Community arm of the Foundation's work. The section has been revised to formally establish two standing program committees — Research and Community — as founding committees in the bylaws. This reflects the board's decision that these two areas are permanent, core lanes of Foundation work, each requiring ongoing board leadership, external relationships, regular reporting, and the ability to develop programs over time. Communications is not established as a standing committee; it is a president-led function governed by board policy. The section also now authorizes working groups beneath any standing committee for specific tasks or projects, which may include non-board members. The Finance Committee and Nominating and Governance Committee remain. The section title has been updated from "Recommended Standing Committees" to "Standing Program Committees" and the introductory language updated from "encouraged to establish as the organization grows" to "hereby establishes."

Before
"The Board is encouraged to establish the following standing committees as the organization grows: (a) Finance Committee (b) Research Committee (c) Nominating and Governance Committee."
After
"The Board hereby establishes the following standing committees... (a) Finance Committee (b) Research Committee (c) Community Committee (d) Nominating and Governance Committee." Working groups authorized beneath each committee.
8
Quorum simplified to a majority of directors then in office
Article II §6

The original draft set quorum at one-third of the board, with a floor of a majority when the board has fewer than six directors. This two-tier formula was complex and created inconsistency as the board grows. The quorum has been simplified to a majority of directors then in office at all board sizes. This is a higher standard than Virginia law requires but ensures meaningful participation at every meeting regardless of board size, and matches the quorum standard used in the Board Policies Handbook.

Before
"At any meeting of the Board, one-third (1/3), but not fewer than a majority when the Board has fewer than six directors, shall constitute a quorum..."
After
"At any meeting of the Board, a majority of directors then in office shall constitute a quorum..."
9
Meeting notice increased from five days to fourteen days
Article II §§8, 9

The original draft required five days notice for both regular and special meetings, which is the Virginia statutory minimum. Fourteen days reflects standard nonprofit governance practice and gives board members — who are volunteers with full-time jobs and family commitments — a realistic window to prepare, review materials, and arrange their schedules. An emergency meeting provision has also been added: in urgent circumstances where fourteen days notice is not practicable, a special meeting may be called with at least forty-eight hours notice, provided a two-thirds majority of all directors vote to proceed and the emergency is documented in the minutes.

Before
"Notice of all regular and special meetings shall be provided to all directors at least five (5) days before the meeting..." No emergency meeting provision.
After
"Notice of all regular and special meetings shall be provided to all directors at least fourteen (14) days before the meeting..." Emergency meetings permitted with 48 hours notice and two-thirds vote.
10
Communications Committee removed; communications designated as a president-led function
Article V §2

The Communications Committee has been removed as a standing committee. At this stage of the Foundation's development, communications work is a board-managed function coordinated by the board president rather than a formal committee structure. Formalizing it as a standing committee would create governance overhead for what is currently a single-person operational function. If the board determines additional communications support is needed, a working group can be established. The standing program committees in the bylaws are now Research Committee, Community Committee, Finance Committee, and Nominating and Governance Committee.

Before
"(d) Communications Committee: implements board-approved messaging, maintains brand consistency across all channels..."
After
Communications Committee removed. Article V §2 now lists (a) Finance Committee (b) Research Committee (c) Community Committee (d) Nominating and Governance Committee. Communications is a president-led function governed by board policy.
11
Secretary duties updated to include agenda and materials distribution
Article III §2(c)

The original Secretary duties did not include distributing the meeting agenda and materials in advance of meetings. Distributing a final agenda and supporting materials at least seven days before each meeting is standard nonprofit governance practice and gives board members adequate time to prepare for meaningful participation. The Secretary duties have been updated to make this responsibility explicit in the bylaws rather than leaving it to informal practice.

Before
"The Secretary shall be responsible for keeping accurate minutes... providing draft minutes to directors at least seven (7) days prior to the subsequent meeting..."
After
"...distributing a final agenda and meeting materials to directors at least seven (7) days before each meeting; providing draft minutes to directors at least seven (7) days prior to the subsequent meeting..."
12
President/Board Chair duties updated to reflect strategic leadership role in absence of Executive Director
Article III §2(a)

The original bylaws described the President/Board Chair's duties in governance terms — presiding over meetings, facilitating board work, and signing documents. The Foundation does not currently have a paid Executive Director, which means the President/Board Chair carries a broader operational and strategic leadership responsibility than the original language reflected. A phrase has been added to clarify that the President/Board Chair provides strategic leadership to ensure the Foundation operates cohesively in the absence of an Executive Director. This language is intentionally scoped to the pre-ED period — once an Executive Director is hired, that role assumes operational and strategic leadership and the President/Board Chair returns to a governance-focused role.

Before
"The President/Board Chair shall preside at all meetings of the Board, facilitate the work of the Board, serve as the chief executive officer of the Corporation in the absence of a paid Executive Director..."
After
"...provides strategic leadership to ensure the Foundation operates cohesively in the absence of an Executive Director..." added to the President/Board Chair duties.
13
Bylaw amendment notice updated to fourteen days; emergency amendment provision removed
Article XI §1

The original draft required ten days notice for standard bylaw amendments and allowed emergency amendments with five days notice and a unanimous vote. The ten-day standard was inconsistent with the fourteen-day notice standard used throughout the rest of the bylaws for meetings. The notice period has been updated to fourteen days for consistency. The emergency amendment provision has been removed — if a truly urgent bylaw change is needed, the board can call an emergency meeting under the emergency meeting provision in Article II Section 8 and proceed from there.

Before
"...at a meeting that has been properly noticed at least ten (10) days before any such change... an amendment may be adopted with a minimum of five (5) days' notice if approved by unanimous vote..."
After
"...at a meeting that has been properly noticed at least fourteen (14) days before any such change..." Emergency amendment provision removed.
