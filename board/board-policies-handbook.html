1 Board Member Roles and Expectations

1.1 General responsibilities

All board members are expected to:

  • Attend all regularly scheduled board meetings and provide advance notice when unable to attend
  • Come prepared to meetings by reviewing materials distributed in advance
  • Actively participate in at least one board committee or program area
  • Respond to communications, email votes, and requests for input within five business days
  • Maintain confidentiality of board discussions, deliberations, and sensitive organizational information
  • Act in the best interest of the Foundation at all times
  • Disclose any conflicts of interest promptly and in accordance with the Conflict of Interest Policy
  • Support board decisions once made, even if they voted against them
  • Represent the Foundation professionally in public settings
  • Support the Foundation's fundraising efforts by participating in board-approved campaigns, helping identify prospective donors or partners, and making a personal financial contribution at a level meaningful to each individual board member

1.2 Committee participation

Each board member is expected to take an active leadership role in at least one of the Foundation's standing program committees or the president-led Communications function. Committee assignments will be determined by board action and reviewed annually.

1.3 Measuring attendance and participation

The board gauges participation based on five areas:

Area What it looks like
Attendance Attending board meetings regularly and giving advance notice when unable to attend. Board members are expected to attend at least 75% of meetings in each calendar year.
Preparation Reviewing materials distributed in advance and coming to meetings ready to discuss the issues at hand.
Engagement Contributing thoughtfully in meetings and committee work, not just being present.
Follow-through Responding to votes and requests within five business days and completing assigned tasks between meetings.
Mission support Actively supporting the Foundation's work through committee leadership or participation, working group involvement, relationship-building, outreach, or other board-approved responsibilities.

The board president will check in informally with each board member as needed throughout the year to discuss how things are going and whether any support or adjustments are needed. Once a year, typically at the end of the calendar year, the board president will have a more comprehensive conversation with each member covering their overall participation, satisfaction with their board experience, and goals for the coming year. Any concerns will be addressed privately and constructively before they become a larger issue.

Participation tracking — including meeting attendance and written consent responses — will be maintained by the secretary and made available to the board president on request.

1.4 Officer roles and responsibilities

The Foundation's officers are the board president, secretary, and treasurer. Their core responsibilities are:

  • President: presides over board meetings, provides strategic leadership across committees to help ensure the Foundation operates as a unified whole, facilitates the board's goal-setting and planning process, coordinates the Foundation's overall direction and priorities in the absence of paid executive staff, conducts board member check-ins, and signs documents on behalf of the Foundation as authorized by the board.
  • Secretary: maintains board records, distributes meeting notices and agendas, prepares and distributes meeting minutes, ensures governance documents are current and accessible.
  • Treasurer: oversees the Foundation's financial records, reporting, and accounts, presents financial reports at each board meeting, manages the annual budget process, ensures timely filing of tax returns and required reports, and reviews and approves reimbursements submitted by the board president.

If an officer is temporarily unavailable, the board president may designate another board member to carry out that officer's time-sensitive duties until the officer is available or a replacement is designated by the board. Permanent vacancies in officer roles shall be filled by board vote as soon as practicable.

1.5 Communication expectations

Board members should direct questions, concerns, and Foundation-related communications through established board channels. Foundation business should not be discussed in informal or public settings outside of authorized board channels without authorization. Any board member who has a substantive conversation with an external party on behalf of the Foundation should log the contact in the board portal.

2 Standing Committees and Working Groups

2.1 Standing committees

The Foundation has two standing program committees established in the bylaws: the Research Committee and the Community Committee. Each committee is the Foundation's primary structure for one of its two core program areas. Committees are led by a board member designated as committee chair, meet regularly, develop external relationships and programs within their area, and report to the full board at each board meeting.

Beneath any standing committee, the chair may organize working groups to carry out specific tasks or projects. Working groups may include non-board members such as volunteers, advisors, or community supporters. Working groups operate under the direction of the committee chair and report through the committee to the full board.

The Foundation also maintains two governance committees established in the bylaws: the Finance Committee, chaired by the treasurer, and the Nominating and Governance Committee. These serve board governance functions and are distinct from the standing program committees.

2.2 Committee leadership

Each standing committee is led by a board member designated as committee chair by the board. Committee chairs are responsible for coordinating committee activity, organizing and overseeing any working groups beneath them, maintaining a log of external contacts and action items in the board portal, and reporting to the full board at each board meeting.

2.3 Committee and working group authority

Committees and working groups may gather information, respond to inquiries, attend informational meetings, and conduct exploratory conversations within their area without prior board approval. Implementing any initiative, program, or commitment requires board approval as described in Section 2.4. Committees and working groups may not bind the Foundation to contracts, public positions, partnerships, financial commitments, or legal obligations unless the board has expressly authorized that authority.

Committees and working groups report to the full board for all significant decisions. Within board-approved programs, they may manage day-to-day execution without requiring board approval for each discrete action, provided all activity remains consistent with board-approved priorities and no binding commitments are made. Any major new initiative, shift in direction, or launch of a new channel or program requires board approval.

2.4 Board approval required

Any new commitment, partnership, program, initiative, or public endorsement involving any of the following must be brought to the full board for approval before the Foundation proceeds:

  • Use of Foundation resources, funds, or volunteer time
  • Public representation of the Foundation's position or support
  • Promotion or advertisement of a research study, program, or external initiative to Foundation constituents
  • Entry into a formal agreement, memorandum of understanding, or collaboration with any external organization
  • Any action that creates a legal or financial obligation for the Foundation
3 Decision-Making and Voting

3.1 Standard voting

The board makes decisions by majority vote at board meetings or via written consent. The board chair facilitates discussion prior to any vote to ensure all perspectives are heard. Votes shall be recorded without commentary to maintain collegiality and respect for differing viewpoints. The outcome of each vote, including any dissenting votes, is documented in the meeting minutes or written consent record.

3.2 Written consent voting

When a decision is time-sensitive or does not warrant convening a full board meeting, the board may act by written consent via email or a shared document in the board portal. For a written consent vote to be valid, all board members must have the opportunity to review and respond, and a majority of seated directors must approve — not just a majority of those who respond. All written consent votes are documented with the date, the item voted on, and each member's response.

3.3 Tabling a decision

Any board member may request that a decision be tabled for further consideration. The board chair may grant a tabling request when additional time for discussion, information gathering, or deliberation would meaningfully improve the decision. When a decision is tabled, the board chair will establish a timeline for revisiting the item. Tabling a decision does not constitute a vote against it.

3.4 Resolving disagreement

Disagreement is a normal and healthy part of board governance. The board chair is responsible for creating space for all perspectives to be heard before a vote is called. Board members are encouraged to raise concerns openly during discussion rather than after a decision is made.

When a vote is taken, the outcome is recorded without commentary. Board members are not expected to explain or defend their vote, and others are not expected to respond to it. Once a decision is made by majority vote, all board members are expected to support it going forward, regardless of how they voted.

If a board member feels strongly that a decision warrants reconsideration, the appropriate path is to raise it at a subsequent meeting with new information or a clear rationale — not to relitigate it informally outside of board channels.

3.5 Revisiting decisions

The board may revisit and revise any prior decision by majority vote. Board members are encouraged to raise concerns or flag new information that may warrant reconsideration of a past decision.

4 Conflict of Interest

4.1 Disclosure

Board members must promptly disclose any actual, potential, or perceived conflict of interest related to any matter before the board. This includes personal, financial, or professional relationships that could influence or appear to influence a board member's judgment. Disclosure should be made to the board chair or secretary prior to any discussion or vote on the matter in question. If a conflict arises during a meeting, the board member shall disclose it at that time before discussion proceeds.

Examples of conflicts that must be disclosed include but are not limited to:

  • A board member owns or has a significant financial stake in a company the Foundation is considering hiring or contracting with
  • A board member's spouse or family member works for an organization the Foundation is considering partnering with or endorsing
  • A board member has a personal or professional relationship with a researcher whose study the Foundation is considering promoting or funding
  • A board member is themselves a researcher who would benefit from Foundation visibility, funding, or endorsement
  • A board member stands to receive compensation, recognition, or career advancement as a result of a Foundation decision
  • A board member sits on the board of another organization that would benefit from a Foundation partnership or referral
  • A board member has a close personal friendship with someone being considered for a paid role or contract with the Foundation
  • A board member has a public advocacy position or organizational affiliation that could conflict with a Foundation stance or partnership decision

This list is illustrative, not exhaustive. When in doubt, disclose.

4.2 Recusal

A board member with a conflict of interest in a matter under discussion must recuse themselves from both the discussion and the vote on that matter. The recused board member does not participate in the discussion or vote on that item. Quorum treatment for a recused member shall follow the bylaws and applicable Virginia law, unless the board adopts a specific procedural rule by resolution. Recusal is noted in the meeting minutes. The board member shall leave the room during deliberations if requested.

4.3 Compensation and related-party transactions

Any arrangement involving payment to a director, officer, immediate family member of a director or officer, or an entity in which a director or officer has a financial interest must be disclosed to the full board before the arrangement is entered into. The interested party must recuse themselves from discussion and the vote. The remaining disinterested directors must determine that the arrangement is fair to the Foundation, consistent with its mission, and in its best interest. The basis for that determination, including any comparable data reviewed, must be documented in the meeting minutes. This applies to compensation, consulting arrangements, contracts for services, and any other transaction that could benefit a related party.

4.4 Annual acknowledgment

Board members will review and sign the Foundation's Conflict of Interest Policy annually, typically at the board's first meeting of the year. Changes in circumstances that create new conflicts should be reported promptly. Signed copies are retained in the Foundation's permanent records.

5 Financial Controls and Accountability

5.1 Bank account authorization

The board shall designate authorized signatories for all Foundation bank accounts by board resolution. Any change to authorized signatories requires a board vote.

5.2 Spending authority

The treasurer is responsible for operating within the board-approved budget. Expenditures within approved budget categories and below a dollar threshold set by board resolution may be made by the treasurer without additional board approval. Any expenditure outside the approved budget or above the threshold set by board resolution requires board approval prior to commitment. The board shall set the spending threshold at or before the first meeting at which financial activity is anticipated.

5.3 Financial reporting

The treasurer shall provide a financial report at each board meeting, including account balances, income, and expenses since the prior meeting. The board shall review annual financial statements before they are finalized.

5.4 Internal controls

The Foundation shall maintain appropriate internal controls to safeguard assets, ensure accurate financial reporting, and prevent fraud or misuse of funds. This includes dual authorization for expenditures above a threshold set by the board and segregation of financial duties where practicable.

5.5 Directors and Officers insurance

The board shall obtain Directors and Officers (D&O) liability insurance before or promptly after the Foundation begins active operations. D&O insurance protects board members from personal liability for good-faith decisions made in their capacity as directors. The board shall review coverage annually to ensure it remains adequate for the Foundation's size and activities. The treasurer is responsible for flagging renewal dates and presenting coverage options to the board for review.

6 Fundraising and Development

6.1 Shared board responsibility

Fundraising is a shared responsibility of the full board. All board members are expected to support the Foundation's fundraising efforts by participating in board-approved campaigns, helping identify prospective donors and partners, and making a personal financial contribution at a level meaningful to them. No individual board member may make commitments to donors, grant funders, or sponsors without board authorization.

6.2 President's coordination role

The board president coordinates the Foundation's fundraising efforts in the absence of paid development staff. This includes identifying grant opportunities, cultivating relationships with prospective donors and funders, and presenting an annual fundraising plan to the board for approval. The president does not execute fundraising alone — board members participate actively in carrying out the approved plan.

6.3 Grant applications

All grant applications must be reviewed and approved by the full board before submission. The board shall confirm that the grant is consistent with the Foundation's mission, that the Foundation has the capacity to fulfill any obligations the grant creates, and that any reporting requirements are understood and manageable. No grant application may be submitted on behalf of the Foundation without prior board approval.

6.4 Fundraising campaigns

All fundraising campaigns — including annual campaigns, event-based fundraising, and online campaigns — require board approval before launch. The board shall review the campaign goal, timeline, messaging, and any donor commitments before the campaign goes live.

6.5 Future development structure

As the Foundation grows, the board may establish a Development Committee or working group to coordinate fundraising efforts. Until that time, fundraising is coordinated by the president with full board participation.

7 Gift Acceptance and Expense Reimbursement

7.1 Gift acceptance

The Foundation welcomes donations that advance its mission. The following guidelines govern what the Foundation will and will not accept.

The Foundation may accept:

  • Cash and check donations
  • Credit card and online donations
  • Donor-advised fund grants
  • Unrestricted gifts of publicly traded securities
  • Memorial and tribute gifts

The following require board approval before acceptance:

  • Gifts with restrictions that fall outside the Foundation's established program areas, that impose conditions the Foundation cannot reasonably fulfill, or that limit the Foundation's discretion in ways inconsistent with its mission
  • Non-cash gifts other than publicly traded securities, including vehicles, equipment, or real property
  • Gifts of cryptocurrency or other digital assets
  • Gifts from donors who may expect something in return, including naming rights, promotional consideration, or influence over Foundation decisions
  • Any gift above a threshold set by board resolution that the board determines warrants additional review

Routine donations — including cash, check, credit card, and online gifts — may be accepted administratively without additional approval. The Foundation reserves the right to decline any gift that is inconsistent with its mission, creates undue administrative burden, or presents legal, financial, or reputational risk. Unusual, restricted, non-cash, or higher-risk gifts require authorization from the board president or the full board as appropriate before acceptance.

7.2 Acknowledgment and documentation

All gifts shall be acknowledged in writing in accordance with IRS requirements. The treasurer is responsible for maintaining accurate donor records and ensuring timely acknowledgment letters are sent. Gifts of $250 or more require a written acknowledgment stating whether any goods or services were provided in exchange.

7.3 Expense reimbursement

Board members and authorized volunteers may be reimbursed for reasonable, necessary expenses incurred on behalf of the Foundation. To receive reimbursement:

  • Expenses must be pre-approved by the board president or treasurer when practicable, or submitted with a brief explanation when pre-approval was not possible
  • Receipts are required for all expenses above $25
  • Reimbursement requests must be submitted within 30 days of the expense
  • The treasurer processes reimbursements and maintains records of all payments

The treasurer reviews and approves all reimbursement requests. The board president's reimbursement requests shall be reviewed and approved by the treasurer. The treasurer's own reimbursement requests shall be reviewed and approved by the board president. No one may approve their own reimbursement.

8 Whistleblower Policy

8.1 Purpose

The Foundation is committed to ethical conduct and legal compliance. Board members and volunteers are encouraged to report in good faith any suspected misconduct, financial irregularity, legal violation, or breach of Foundation policy. This policy protects those who raise concerns in good faith and establishes a clear process for handling reports.

8.2 What to report

Reports under this policy may include but are not limited to:

  • Suspected fraud, theft, or misuse of Foundation funds or assets
  • Falsification of financial records, reports, or grant applications
  • Violations of law or regulations applicable to the Foundation
  • Undisclosed conflicts of interest
  • Harassment or retaliation against a board member or volunteer
  • Any other conduct that appears to violate the Foundation's policies or ethical standards

8.3 How to report

Concerns should be reported to the board president. If the concern involves the board president, it should be reported to the secretary or treasurer. Reports may be made verbally or in writing. The Foundation will not require a reporter to identify themselves, though providing contact information may assist in investigating the concern.

8.4 Non-retaliation

No board member or volunteer who reports a concern in good faith shall be subject to retaliation, harassment, or adverse treatment of any kind as a result of making a report. Any person who retaliates against a good-faith reporter is subject to disciplinary action up to and including removal from the board.

8.5 Investigation and documentation

The board president, or another designated board member if the president is implicated, shall be responsible for investigating all reports promptly and in good faith. The nature of the report, the investigation conducted, and the outcome shall be documented and retained in accordance with the Foundation's Document Retention and Destruction Policy. If a report involves potential legal liability, the board shall consult legal counsel before proceeding.

9 Form 990 Review

9.1 Board review required

The full board shall receive and review a complete copy of the Foundation's IRS Form 990 before it is filed each year. The treasurer shall distribute the draft Form 990 to all board members at least fourteen days before the filing deadline to allow adequate time for review.

9.2 Review process

Board members are encouraged to raise any questions or concerns about the Form 990 prior to filing. The board president or treasurer shall address any questions and document that the review was completed. The date of board review and the names of board members who participated shall be noted in the board records.

9.3 Public disclosure

The Foundation shall make its Form 990 available for public inspection as required by law, including posting it on the Foundation's website or a public database within a reasonable time after filing.

10 Communications and Public Representation

10.1 Official spokesperson

The board president serves as the default coordinator for external inquiries unless the board designates another representative for a particular matter. No board member may make public statements or commitments on behalf of the Foundation unless authorized by board policy or board action. Committee chairs and other board members may respond to routine inbound inquiries, attend informational meetings, and communicate in an exploratory capacity within their area, provided they do not make commitments, state positions the board has not approved, or imply Foundation endorsement where none has been authorized. Substantive external contacts should be logged in the board portal.

10.2 Brand and messaging consistency

All external communications must adhere to the Foundation's brand standards and approved messaging. Board members and committee chairs should coordinate with the board president before releasing any external-facing content that represents the Foundation.

10.3 Social media and digital platforms

Only authorized individuals may post to the Foundation's official social media accounts, website, or other public platforms. Personal posts that reference or represent the Foundation should be consistent with board-approved messaging. Confidential information should never be shared online.

11 Records and Documentation

11.1 Board portal

The Foundation maintains an internal board portal that serves as the central repository for governance documents, meeting minutes, financial records, committee reports, and the contact log. All board members are expected to use the portal or email for official Foundation communications and documentation.

11.2 Contact log

Any board member or committee chair who has a substantive conversation with an external party on behalf of the Foundation must log the contact in the board portal within a reasonable time. The log entry should include the date, contact name and organization, nature of the discussion, and any action items or follow-up required.

11.3 Digital access and account security

All Foundation-owned digital accounts, platforms, and credentials are organizational assets, not personal ones. The following standards apply:

  • All Foundation account credentials must be stored in a board-approved secure password manager or credential system, not in personal accounts or devices exclusively.
  • When a board member's role changes or their service ends, access to Foundation accounts must be removed promptly — no later than seven days after the change.
  • Official Foundation records must not exist solely in a personal email account, personal cloud storage, or personal device. All official records should be stored in the board portal or another board-designated system.
  • No individual may retain exclusive control over any Foundation-owned digital asset, account, or system. At least two authorized individuals must have access to each critical Foundation account at all times.

The board president is responsible for overseeing compliance with this section. Any suspected unauthorized access or security breach should be reported to the board immediately.

11.4 Public transparency

The Foundation is committed to transparency with donors, grantors, families, and the public beyond the legal minimum. The Foundation will make its IRS Form 990 publicly available within a reasonable time after filing, maintain a public-facing website that accurately describes its mission and programs, and provide donors with acknowledgment letters that include information on how their contributions support the Foundation's work. The board shall periodically review what information is publicly disclosed and ensure it reflects the Foundation's actual activities and finances.

11.5 Document retention

Records shall be retained in accordance with the Foundation's Document Retention and Destruction Policy. Foundational documents — including the articles of incorporation, bylaws, tax-exempt determination letter, meeting minutes, conflict of interest disclosures, and adopted policies — shall be retained permanently. No record subject to a pending or anticipated legal matter or government inquiry may be destroyed.

12 Board Member Conduct and Confidentiality

12.1 Conduct standards

All board members are expected to conduct themselves with integrity and professionalism. This includes treating others with respect, refraining from personal attacks or harassment, and addressing disagreements constructively.

12.2 Confidentiality

Board members shall maintain the confidentiality of sensitive Foundation information, including financial data, legal matters, strategic plans under development, and donor and family information. This obligation continues after a board member's tenure ends.

12.3 Board communications between meetings

Official board business shall be conducted at properly noticed board meetings or through written consent procedures. Informal communications through personal messaging applications, social media, or other unofficial channels do not constitute board action and may not be used to make or record board decisions. Board members may use unofficial channels for scheduling and general coordination, but any matter requiring a board decision must be handled through formal channels.

13 Meeting Procedures

13.1 Frequency and notice

The board shall meet no fewer than four times per calendar year. Board members shall receive written notice of each meeting at least fourteen days in advance. A final agenda and any materials to be discussed shall be distributed at least seven days before the meeting.

13.2 Emergency meetings

In urgent circumstances where fourteen days notice is not practicable — such as a legal matter, financial emergency, or time-sensitive decision — a special meeting may be called with at least forty-eight hours notice provided to all board members by email or telephone. A two-thirds majority of all directors then in office must vote to proceed. The nature of the emergency shall be stated in the notice and recorded in the minutes. Emergency meetings should be used sparingly and only when the matter genuinely cannot wait for a regularly noticed meeting or written consent vote.

13.3 Quorum

A quorum shall consist of a majority of seated board members. No binding business shall be conducted without a quorum present. If a quorum is not present, the meeting may proceed informally for discussion purposes only.

13.4 Minutes

The secretary shall prepare draft minutes and distribute them to board members at least seven days before the subsequent meeting for review and approval. Minutes shall include the date, attendees, topics discussed, actions taken, conflicts of interest disclosed, and all votes with results recorded.

13.5 Remote participation

Board members may participate in meetings by telephone, video conference, or other electronic means. Remote participation counts toward quorum and attendance requirements.

14 Amendments and Policy Review

14.1 Amendment process

These policies may be amended by a majority vote of the board at any duly noticed meeting, provided that notice of the proposed amendment and its text were distributed to all board members at least seven days in advance. Policies may also be amended by written consent as described in Section 3.2. All amendments are documented and the board portal is updated to reflect the current version upon adoption.

14.2 Annual review

The board shall review these policies annually and recommend any updates needed to reflect changes in the Foundation's operations, applicable law, or governance best practices.
